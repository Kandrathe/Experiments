# Psalm 2 - Quare Fremuerunt Gentes

## 1. Original Text (KJV)

1 Why do the heathen rage, and the people imagine a vain thing?

2 The kings of the earth set themselves, and the rulers take counsel together, against the Lord, and against his anointed, saying,

3 Let us break their bands asunder, and cast away their cords from us.

4 He that sitteth in the heavens shall laugh: the Lord shall have them in derision.

5 Then shall he speak unto them in his wrath, and vex them in his sore displeasure.

6 Yet have I set my king upon my holy hill of Zion.

7 I will declare the decree: the LORD hath said unto me, Thou art my Son; this day have I begotten thee.

8 Ask of me, and I shall give thee the heathen for thine inheritance, and the uttermost parts of the earth for thy possession.

9 Thou shalt break them with a rod of iron; thou shalt dash them in pieces like a potter's vessel.

10 Be wise now therefore, O ye kings: be instructed, ye judges of the earth.

11 Serve the LORD with fear, and rejoice with trembling.

12 Kiss the Son, lest he be angry, and ye perish from the way, when his wrath is kindled but a little. Blessed are all they that put their trust in him.

---

## 2. What David Is Saying

### Situation
The nations and their rulers unite to reject the LORD and his anointed king.

### Emotional State
Urgency and awe, moving from the rulers’ defiance to a warning that still offers refuge.

### Conflict
Rulers mistake freedom from God’s rule for power, while the LORD has already established his king.

### Turning Point
God declares his king on Zion; the later address calls rulers to wisdom and service.

### Core Spiritual Truth
Earthly power does not overturn God’s decree; those who trust in him are blessed.

---

## 3. My Interpretation

### Contemporary Equivalent
Public leaders and communities chase power as if they answer to no higher authority.

### What I Preserved
The raging nations, their futile plans, heaven’s response, Zion’s king, the call to serve, and the final refuge.

### Modern Images / Language
Fleeting crowns and shadowed plans make the conflict singable; the Latin title remains an invocation of the opening question.

### Song Thesis
The song asks wandering hearts to trade vain power for reverence and refuge in the King.

In His arms, they find their peace, where they’ve belonged all along.

Lift your eyes to Zion’s King, His grace will carry you. (Will carry you...)

## 6. Production

**Working Title:** Quare Fremuerunt Gentes  
**Genre:** Spiritual acoustic ballad with a regal swell  
**Tempo:** Not documented; adjust to the existing phrasing  
**Key:** D major (draft intro)  
**Vocal:** Gentle lead with an optional spoken intro and layered chorus  
**Instrumentation:** Fingerpicked acoustic guitar; restrained bass, percussion and sustained harmonies as the song grows  
**Mood:** Awe, warning, invitation  
**Emotional Arc:** Questions and defiance give way to declaration, warning, and refuge  

### Suno Style
Acoustic spiritual ballad in D major; intimate opening and a gradual regal lift; keep the Latin title phrase prominent and intelligible.

### Arrangement Notes
Use the existing D–G–A–Bm intro cue. Let Zion’s declaration gain weight, drop back for the warning, then broaden the final chorus.

### Vocal Notes
Treat the written phonetic variants of the title as performance notes; choose one consistent pronunciation when recording.

### Mix / Master Notes
Keep the opening voice close and the final chorus broad without masking the consonants in the title phrase.
